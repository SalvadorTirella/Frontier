# GAME DESIGN & VISUAL DIRECTION BRIEF

## PROJECT OVERVIEW

We are creating a **premium 3D tactical arcade army battle game for mobile and desktop**, built with Babylon.js.

The game combines:

* army building
* tactical lane management
* autonomous combat
* real-time battles
* wave survival
* strategic positioning
* unit specialization
* weapon progression
* heroes
* defensive structures
* airstrikes and special attacks
* terrain advantages
* cover
* bosses
* base destruction
* territory conquest
* account progression

The game should feel like a **commercial premium mobile game**, not like a browser prototype.

The player should be able to watch a gameplay video and think:

> "This looks like a professionally produced 3D game."

They should NOT think:

> "This is a Babylon.js / HTML game."

Babylon.js is the underlying technology, but the technology itself should never define the visual identity.

---

# 1. CORE FANTASY

The central fantasy is:

## "BUILD AN ARMY, COMMAND THE BATTLEFIELD, AND WATCH IT GROW INTO AN OVERWHELMING WAR MACHINE."

The player starts with a very small force.

Example:

```text
3 SOLDIERS
1 BASIC RIFLE
$100
```

During a battle, the player progressively builds an army:

```text
3 soldiers
↓
8 soldiers
↓
15 soldiers
↓
30 soldiers
↓
specialized units
↓
heroes
↓
heavy weapons
↓
tanks
↓
defensive structures
↓
airstrikes
↓
massive army
↓
BOSS
```

The game should create a strong sense of **escalation**.

The battlefield should visually evolve from:

> "small military squad"

into:

> "large-scale military force."

---

# 2. THE PLAYER'S ROLE

The player does NOT directly control individual soldiers.

The soldiers fight autonomously.

The player acts as the battlefield commander.

The player decides:

* which units to buy
* which lane receives them
* when to reinforce
* when to move units
* which weapon upgrades to select
* which heroes to deploy
* where to place defensive structures
* where to use special attacks
* which enemies to prioritize
* how to distribute the army
* how to react to losses
* how to manage the battle economy

The player is therefore simultaneously:

### COMMANDER

and

### ARMY BUILDER

and

### STRATEGIST.

---

# 3. BATTLEFIELD

The initial battlefield contains:

## THREE VERTICAL LANES

Viewed from an isometric camera.

Conceptually:

```text
             ENEMY SIDE

        👾          👾
        ↓           ↓

    ║ LANE 1 ║ LANE 2 ║ LANE 3 ║

    ║         ║        ║        ║
    ║         ║        ║        ║
    ║         ║        ║        ║
    ║         ║        ║        ║

    🪖🪖      🔫🔫     🛡️

             PLAYER
```

However, THREE LANES are only the initial configuration.

The visual/world design must eventually support:

* 1 lane
* 2 lanes
* 3 lanes
* 4 lanes
* 5+ lanes
* branching lanes
* merging lanes
* different battlefield topologies
* different maps
* different environments

Do NOT visually design the game around the assumption that every map permanently has three identical lanes.

# 4. CAMERA

The primary gameplay camera is:

## ISOMETRIC

The camera should provide:

* strong battlefield readability
* cinematic perspective
* clear unit silhouettes
* readable formations
* readable lanes
* visible projectiles
* visible explosions
* visible structures
* clear enemy approach

The camera should feel closer to a premium mobile strategy/action game than a traditional RTS.

It must make the battlefield look dramatic while preserving gameplay readability.

# 6. THE ARMY

The army is composed of physical 3D units.

Initial player units include:

* Soldier / Infantry
* Rifleman
* Heavy
* RPG
* Flamethrower
* Sniper
* Grenadier
* Tank

Future units can include many additional classes.

The army should not look like identical clones.

Use a modular visual system:

```text
BASE SOLDIER
+
UNIFORM
+
ARMOR
+
HELMET
+
WEAPON
+
ACCESSORIES
+
FACTION
+
LEVEL / VARIANT
```

This allows a large army to feel varied without requiring completely unique character models for every soldier.

# 8. HEROES

Heroes are special units.

They are NOT manually controlled.

They fight autonomously.

Each hero has a unique battlefield role.

Examples:

## MEDIC HERO

Provides healing.

Visual identity:

* medical equipment
* recognizable medical markings
* support-oriented silhouette

Gameplay fantasy:

> "Keep my army alive."

---

## ASSAULT HERO

Extremely powerful personal damage.

Visual identity:

* elite military equipment
* stronger armor
* visually dominant silhouette
* powerful weapon

Gameplay fantasy:

> "This unit can turn a losing lane around."

---

## SUPPORT HERO

Provides an aura/buff.

Example:

```text
+20% FIRE RATE
TO NEARBY FRIENDLY UNITS
```

The visual language should communicate that the hero is affecting nearby soldiers.

# 11. COMBAT

Combat is autonomous.

Soldiers:

* detect enemies
* choose targets
* aim
* fire
* reload
* react to hits
* move
* die
* reposition
* change lanes when capable

Combat should feel highly active.

The battlefield should constantly communicate:

* bullets
* tracers
* muzzle flashes
* impacts
* explosions
* smoke
* dust
* debris
* blood where enabled
* enemy reactions
* damage numbers
* deaths
* movement

The player should always feel that something is happening.

---

# 12. PROJECTILES

Small-arms projectiles should look:

* fast
* small
* believable
* cinematic

Use subtle tracers where appropriate.

Heavy projectiles should be much more visually pronounced.

Examples:

### RIFLE

Small fast tracer.

### SNIPER

Fast, precise, powerful projectile.

### RPG

Visible rocket with smoke trail.

### GRENADE

Physical arc.

### MISSILE

Large projectile with propulsion effects.

### AIRSTRIKE

Aircraft/missile/ordinance sequence with large explosion.

The visual language must immediately communicate weapon power.

# 13. DAMAGE AND DEATH

Damage feedback is highly important.

A hit can produce:

```text
IMPACT
+
STAGGER
+
DAMAGE NUMBER
+
SOUND
+
VFX
+
REACTION
```

Death:

```text
HIT
↓
REACTION
↓
STAGGER
↓
DEATH ANIMATION
↓
BODY FALLS
↓
FORMATION REORGANIZES
```

The death of a soldier should matter visually.

The player should be able to see that their army is suffering losses.

---

# 14. BLOOD

Blood intensity is configurable:

```text
NONE
NORMAL
EXTREME
```

### NONE

No blood.

### NORMAL

Moderate combat feedback.

### EXTREME

Highly spectacular blood effects.

The art direction must support all three without changing the underlying animation system.

---

# 15. FORMATIONS

Units physically occupy formation positions.

They are NOT floating counters.

Example:

```text
       SNIPER
          ↓

   🔫 🔫 🔫 🔫
    🛡️ 🛡️ 🛡️
   🔫 🔫 🔫 🔫
```

Different classes can occupy different tactical positions.

Possible conceptual arrangement:

```text
FRONT
Heavy
Infantry

MIDDLE
Rifleman
Grenadier
Flamethrower

BACK
Sniper
RPG
```

The visual result should resemble an actual small military force.

When units die or are removed:

```text
formation reorganizes
```

This should happen smoothly.

---

# 16. LANE TRANSITIONS

Some units can physically move between lanes.

This must be visually clear.

### Infantry

Walks toward another lane.

### Sniper

Leaves position, stands, moves, crosses, establishes firing position again.

### Rifleman

Normally cannot cross unless the map has a valid bifurcation.

### Heavy

Requires a valid physical connection.

### Hero

Can perform a visually spectacular jump.

Lane movement should feel like a real battlefield maneuver.

### 1. ARMY BUILDING

# 38. IMPORTANT CREATIVE PRINCIPLE

Do not design isolated assets.

Everything must belong to a coherent:

# VISUAL BIBLE

For example, if designing a soldier, consider:

* what environment he belongs in
* what lighting affects him
* what materials his uniform uses
* what weapon he carries
* what animations he needs
* how he looks next to a Heavy
* how he looks next to a Tank
* how he looks next to an Alien
* how he looks in a formation of 50 soldiers
* how he looks on a phone screen
* how he looks during explosions
* how he looks during boss combat

Likewise, when designing an environment, consider:

* unit scale
* lane readability
* cover
* camera
* lighting
* VFX
* destruction
* gameplay
* performance

Everything must be designed as part of one game.
